# Changelog

0.22.0: no room is walled off. A room's accent wall is the tone its wall run carries from corner to corner instead of a facing plane laid over the wall, which used to stand across the doorways of the neighbouring room (1661 of 8157 interior doors in city-urbe-small were sealed by one) and z-fight the wall face it sat inside. A door whose shared stretch is cut away by the outline is dropped so the reachability pass can cut a working one, and every doorway is checked against the built mesh before the GLB is written: nothing may stand in the clear opening (E_UNREACHABLE_SPACE names the door and the material blocking it). All 71 parcels of the small city build with every interior doorway open.
0.21.4: the core block slides along its band and its row scan starts under the roof housing the exterior published, so the stair head lands in the roof cutout.
0.21.3: door casings around every doorway and window casings on the room side of every window.
0.21.2: doors are refitted into the stretch their rooms share after the wall passes (no walled-off rooms); door heads at 2.5 m and 3 m on the grid.
0.21.1: under a curtain wall the ceiling meets the spandrel line; partitions may stand on pane mullions.
0.21.0: the interior grid: partitions snap onto a 0.5 m grid from the outline corner (a quarter tile at most, never into an opening or off the core), and floors and ceilings tile from that corner so the pattern runs whole through the building and meets the exterior window borders.
0.20.6: unit-mapped faces follow glTF v (0 at the top), so wall screens and lift panels are upright as well as unmirrored; framed wall art carries a picture (an image ad of the theme) inside a 6 cm wood border.
0.20.5: unit-mapped side faces (wall screens, lift door panels) read left to right from their front.
0.20.4: a partition landing on an opening's boundary stands on the frame member there, so on a curtain wall the walls fall on the mullion lines and never mid-pane.

0.20.3: a stair shaft's downlight hangs under the mid landing, half a storey up at the far end from the entry, so nothing floats in the stair void.

0.20.2: a spot fixture asks for the materials box's `lamp` variant (recessed lens and housing) instead of a flat lit panel, so a downlight reads as a lamp.

0.20.1: wall fields are plain plaster everywhere; a pattern class is asked for on the accent band and the ceiling only, so it reads as a border and never as a wall of hexagons.

0.20: reveals keep out of the corner walls. An opening's hole closes from either end where its reveal, square to its own edge from the skin clearance back to the wall depth, would stand in a neighbouring edge's wall: a curtain-wall bay 0.12 m from a right-angle corner is lined from the wall depth in, and before an acute apex the last wallDepth / tan(half the corner angle) of the opening is closed (0.31 m at p53's 59-degree apex), so the walls meeting at a corner own their depth and the lining ring turns the corner behind both. The shell fit check reads the same rule: a reveal vertex inside another edge's wall strip is `E_SHELL_BREACH`, and the error names the floor that holds the vertex, the upper one on a slab line. Proven on city parcel p53 (basement, 59-degree apex, bays to 0.11 m of it), kept as a fixture blueprint; `makeFixture` takes such a `blueprint` and a string seed.

0.19: anchors are judged where they are read, the gate names what a plate misses, floors ship as their own GLBs. An NPC anchor walked out of a doorway is tested at the centimetre position that is exported, so a cell centre on a door zone's edge never rounds back into it (city parcels p8, p15, p33, p68 and tiny p6 assemble; p15 is the fixture test). `coreFeasibility` reports `plateDepth` and `plateDepthFloor` (the shallowest floor across the frame, setbacks and basements included, since the core is identical on every floor), `minCrossDepth` and `minCompactDepth` (the depth the compact core's stair columns need behind the corridor face), `compactDepthOk` (the exact per-floor column test) and `blocker` (`cross_depth`, `band`, `compact_depth` or `walkup_floors`, the nearest miss); `E_FLOOR_TOO_SMALL` messages name the same condition. `floorGlbs: true` (`--floor-glbs`) writes each floor band's interior as `floors/NNN.glb` next to its JSON, same materials, node scheme and texture mode as `building.glb`, the bands together being exactly its interior meshes. The shell wall depth reads `facade.wallDepth` from the blueprint when the exterior publishes it and the per-style table otherwise.

0.18: the interior fits inside the shell. The shell wall is modelled by facade style (its skin on the outline, reveals and units reaching 0.15 m in on curtain-wall and glass, 0.3 m on panel, 0.5 m on megablock), and everything the interior emits stays behind that depth: the facade lining is the ring between the plate at that depth and the plate one lining deeper (exact insets, so a tiny facade step or a sharp corner never pulls it toward the skin), partitions are cut flush with it whatever the facade's angle, slabs and ceilings stop at its outer face, and the vertical core, furniture, light housings and the walkable grid keep to the plate behind it. Each opening's hole sits 0.01 m inside the shell's opening and is lined back to 0.02 m behind the skin, so no cavity shows and no interior face shares a plane with the shell's reveal. Every vertex is checked before the GLB is written (`E_SHELL_BREACH`), and the fixture sweep proves it on off-axis footprints, an acute-cornered city parcel, a curtain-wall shell and megablock reveals. The core-feasibility recipe reads the inset plates (step 0), so the gate and the generator still agree.

0.17: lighting reads as lighting. Fixtures publish `facing`: a strip or a spot throws down over the room, a cove sits under the ceiling and washes it upward, which is what puts light on the ceiling plane; the cove housing gains the lip that hides its lamp. Strips in a row nearly abut, so an aisle reads as one continuous line rather than a dotted one, downlights read as flush ceiling panels, corridors gain a cove line of their own, and corridors, homes and service rooms carry more flux. The preview lights coves from above their lip, so what it shows is the wash the engine will build.

0.16: the preview shows the lit room. The floor editor instantiates the floor's own fixtures (colour from their temperature, reach from their range and spread) and drops the daylight, and `eye view` stands the camera in a room at head height looking across it. The disk loader is now pulled in only when a run actually reads the materials database, so the browser build no longer trips over node builtins and the preview opens again.

0.15: wall detailing. Every wall is layered from the floor up: a trim baseboard, a dado band in the accent tone to 1.05 m, the patterned field, and a trim band under the ceiling, each proud of the one below so the junctions catch light. One wall per room is faced in the accent tone, picked by seed and never a facade or a wall carrying a door, so a room reads as colour-blocked rather than four identical planes. The facade lining carries the same bands between its windows. Wall bands are capped only where an end is actually seen (the underside of a lintel, the top of a spandrel), which drops the hidden faces that used to z-fight between stacked bands.

0.14: walls and ceilings take the materials pattern class. A material may now carry `extras.materialVariant`, the variant of its entry the interior wants; the material name stays the plain `theme/kind/tier` key, so a consumer that resolves keys itself is unaffected. Ceilings move to the `ceiling` entry (panel and plain, both drawn), walls ask for a hex field in venues and lobbies, a panel grid in offices and service rooms and two-tone in homes, and light housings ask for the lit strip or the lit panel diffuser. Floors, wood, tile and concrete keep their photographed sets, where real texture earns its place.

0.13: NPC anchors keep out of doorways. The keep-clear rule now covers anchors as well as furniture: a standing role whose ideal spot falls in a door swing, a threshold or the 1.5 m approach inside a street entrance is walked out to the nearest clear reachable cell, and entrance anchors stand 1.7 m inside the opening. Checked as the NPC support is built, so no floor ships with a staffer blocking a door.

0.12: real furniture. Every piece is built from its own parts: tables on legs, chairs with a back, side posts and a seat pad, task chairs with armrests and a star base, bar stools with a footrest, counters with panelled fronts, worktop overhangs and kick recesses, shelving and racks stocked board by board, wardrobes and fridges with door lines and handles, crates in back rooms. Rooms carry seeded small objects: bottles and glasses on bars and service tops, a cup and papers on a desk, a centrepiece on a diner table, stock on every shelf. Chairs are pulled up to tables and desks, stools to the customer side of a counter, and the `seat` anchor sits ON the seat, so a seated NPC lands on the chair (pull-in seating is not a nav obstacle, so the anchor stays on a walkable cell). New additive furniture kinds: `wall_shelf`, `display_screen`, `wall_art` and `crate`, with an optional `elevation` for wall pieces. Light fixtures publish `beamDeg` and `diffuse`, so a strip or a cove instantiates as a wide soft line wash rather than a point glow, and a cove's useful range follows the wall it washes.

0.11: doorways stay clear. Every door, street entrance included, keeps the leaf swing plus 1 m of approach on both sides over the full head height, in one keep-clear rule the furnishing pass and the nav validation share. Reachability repair adds doors after furnishing, so the validator drops anything left standing in a new doorway and reads the grid back with that space open, then machine-checks that no door is blocked before returning the floor.

0.10: stairs the player fits through. Published minimums (schemas/core-feasibility.json): 1.0 m clear flight width and 2.1 m headroom over every tread and landing, both enforced on the whole run of every stair or the build fails with E_UNREACHABLE_SPACE. The walk line is continuous: each floor's arrival landing is real geometry (the top floor included), landings reach the flights they serve, and both flights of a turn share one stretch of run so landings line up storey over storey. The shaft is sized off the worst climb of the blueprint rather than its tallest floor, since a short storey uses fewer, longer flights; the tread slab thins on low storeys and landings give up depth before a climb splits into more flights. Regression test walks the full run of a generated tower, basement to roof, on both stairs.

0.9: a plate whose fitting core rectangle is skewed off its longest edge builds on a rotated frame. When the principal frame holds no core, frames every 5 degrees over a half turn are tried with the same entrance flip and the best fitting one wins (standard before compact before walkup, longer band breaking ties); `frameAngleDeg` reports the frame the answer used and the floors carry it as `coreAngleDeg`. Parcels the principal frame already serves never enter the sweep, so their layout is unchanged. Gate and generator share the frame-and-placement decision, so they still agree. City sample city-urbe-small: all 66 parcels assemble with interiors, p14 (walkup), p53 and p64 (standard) included.

0.8: partitions land on the piers between the facade's windows and doors. Every interior wall line whose end falls inside an opening slides to the nearest clear position, within 2 m and never past a room's minimum span; rooms stay adjacent and doors stay on their walls. A wall locked to a shaft face, or one reaching two facades whose window rhythms share no pier, takes the position with the fewest crossings and is reported as unmovable, so a second pass over a finished floor moves nothing.

0.7: interiors carry light. Every floor JSON publishes a `lights` array: one fixture per light source with kind (strip, spot, cove), 3D position, run length and angle, intensity in lumens, colour temperature and useful range, so the engine instantiates real lights. Every room, corridor and stair shaft is lit by its kind: work strips over offices and kitchens, warm spots in homes, strips plus wall-ceiling cove lines in venues, downlights along corridors and in stairwells. The GLB carries the matching emissive housing at each pose under the `light-fixture` material key. Low storeys keep 2.1 m clear under the dropped ceiling instead of the full service void.

0.6: the default export is a finished furnished textured interior. Every material key resolves through ../materials into real maps (basecolor, normal, occlusion, emission, with the entry's metallic and roughness factors, transmission and IOR for glass); tiled maps carry a KHR_texture_transform of 1 / tiling worldSize over world-meter UVs, exact placements carry 0..1 UVs over their face. Maps ship as external URIs against a configurable base path, `--embed` packs them into one self-contained GLB, `--keys-only` keeps the keys for a consumer that resolves them itself, and a missing materials database degrades to keys so the box still runs standalone. The preview serves the database at /materials and shows a finished building at first load.

Venue programs for commerce and mall floors: `retail` fills the floor with one sales floor (checkout counter, wall shelving, display rack aisles) plus stockroom and staff toilets, `mall_floor` lines a concourse with shop units, each a sales floor with its own counter and back stock room, public toilets off the core. Both staff a `clerk` per sales floor, homed at its counter, with min and max counts from the shelving it holds and a routine of counter service and restocking. Atlas floor slugs pick programs verbatim, so restaurant, coffee shop, shop and mall floors of a mixed building get their own program whatever the building type is. Additive schema values: room kinds `sales_floor` and `concourse`, furniture `display_rack`, role `clerk`, animation `work_stock`, assignment kinds `retail` and `mall_floor`, error `E_MATERIAL_UNRESOLVED`.

The corridor band scan and the core fit check share one coverage test with a relative epsilon, so a band the gate approves always holds the core it places (parcel p65: the band ended on a diagonal corner the scan read as covered and the fit check did not). Compact mode validates its stair columns against the real block rect on every floor instead of the outline's bounding box, the block span arithmetic lives in one function both `coreFeasibility` and `planCore` call, and core fit errors name the rect and its frame coordinates.

0.5: the corridor position scans for a fitting band (taper plates put the core on the fat side, so parcels like a triangle with a narrow apex stay standard), and a compact mode turns stairs into columns reaching the rear strip so near-miss bands keep their elevators. Feasibility surface publishes modes standard|compact|walkup|none with per-mode thresholds and the scan recipe; stair flights follow the shaft's long dimension either way.

0.4: tight footprints degrade to stair-only walkups (mode and floor cap published in the feasibility surface; gate messages quote the recipe's numbers). Shallow plates go single-loaded (core row flush to the back facade) so units keep real room depth; strips too shallow for an entry band become single-room studios with shared floor WCs in the flanking segments. Staffing on constrained parcels restored: furniture prefers doorless walls, furniture anchors sit at reachable mount points with an opposite-side fallback, anchor positions snap to corridor-reached cells, entrance anchors exist only at street level, and reachability repair probes varied door positions instead of stacking duplicates.

0.3: core-feasibility surface published: coreFeasibility(blueprint) export plus schemas/core-feasibility.json (constants and recipe) so the assembler pre-checks parcels; elevator demand clamps to the real core band, so narrow-band tall parcels get fewer cars instead of failing. Strip segments with no corridor contact (e.g. behind the inline egress stair) become sealed service voids: walled, slabbed, nav-blocked, listed under core shafts, never unreachable rooms.

0.2: rotated city parcels supported natively: layout runs in a principal-axis frame (longest street edge, entrance-facing), walls and facade lining at true angles, coreAngleDeg on floor JSONs, world-aligned nav grids unchanged for consumers. Atlas vocabulary verbatim on the contract surface (13 parcel types, tiers poor/mid/rich/high_rich, string seeds hashed internally). Preview loads real engine output. Validated end to end on parcel p1640 (19 floors, rotated decagon): all 1579 anchors route from the entrance.

0.1: full deterministic pipeline: validated request in, completed GLB plus per-floor JSONs plus npc.json out. Vertical core with walkable U-return stairs and elevators on every floor, per-kind floor templates with seeded variance, furniture placeholders, wall-aware nav grid with flood-validated reachability, reference pathfinder, browser preview with a standalone floor editor. Consumes exterior blueprint v0.3 (basements, 12 m floors, floor:N/slab node naming); materials referenced as theme/kind/tier keys.
