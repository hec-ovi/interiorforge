# Industrial service interior

The reference is the industrial tier and the service layers of the William Hare
building and Clouds: service spaces within residential and hospitality buildings,
not a factory survey. The workshop/dispatch program is an adaptation of that
vocabulary to factory use. Key decisions are broad worn mineral backing, sparse
meaningful divisions, independent service pipes, collars, suspension frames,
layered shallow electrical housings, and supplies concentrated at room edges.

The entry's mechanical room reserves its middle for receiving and through access.
Workshop tables occur only in non-entry rooms of at least 48 square metres, with
2.1 m planner aisles. All fittings use the shared room placer's existing exterior,
door-sweep, polygon, hole and circulation reservations. Storage is held on racks;
floor cases remain near walls.

The 0.5 m grid is a construction unit. It is not visible wall tiling. Continuous
service trunks stretch; collars and supports retain their authored dimensions and
repeat at independent metre intervals. Services drop at most 0.269 m below the
ceiling and are omitted below 2.38 m ceiling height or in narrow remainder strips.

All overrides fit the planner's furniture bounds and use existing published
materials. Unknown seat modules use the unchanged consumer's 0.49 m seat support;
the bench remains the existing `fit-bench`, whose support the consumer knows.
`fit-`, `wall-`, `floor-`, and `ceiling-services` prefixes retain existing runtime
occupancy, room-enclosure, surface and collision classification.

## Fittings

The fittings use 3 mm formed metal chamfers, open L-section rack uprights,
folded shelf lips, case latches and taped cartons, smooth circular pipe sections,
fixed-size collars and hangers, and branch runs to service terminals. Electrical
drive banks keep their vented faces and controls above a protective plinth. Their
short conduit loops join the common trunk. Petrochemical green painted enamel is
`cyberpunk/interior-service-enamel/poor#petrol`; it is an existing published PBR
variant. Cabinets and drawers expose their real handles rather than hiding the
hardware behind an opaque front. The task chair has shaped cushions, bent tubular
back supports and cylindrical casters within its navigation footprint.

Exterior is generated first, and Interior consumes the resulting exact shell and
openings with factory use. No fixed dimension is substituted into the interior
planner.

## Equipment, services and bays

`equipment.ts` stands unequal service boxes on an asymmetrical mounting frame, with
unequal box depths, actual hinges and quarter-turn fasteners, an occupied recessed
terminal rail, radiused conduit bundles and a protected cable sag. The removed
maintenance cover is a static detail, not an advertised interactive door. A circular
fan/housing/grille shape is adapted as an indoor ventilation bank; its circular
openings are actual geometry, with recessed blades, separate wire guard, folded
louvers, raised feet, drain pan and condensate outlet.

`service-recipes.ts` adds thick and thin pipes, six cable strands, bolted flanges
and fixed-pitch tray rungs. Narrow strips retain a compact route. `feeds.ts` joins
actual equipment to an existing same-room overhead tray; excluded room holes and
low horizontal paths reject the feed. All pipework remains separate from broad
wall fields. Service metal and vinyl use the finer gunmetal and umber PBR variants.

`bays.ts` organizes large halls into work and rack bays around a four-metre arrival
spine and 8 × 6 m receiving pocket. It respects complete room coverage before
asking the shared collision-aware placer to accept each physical piece, so a large
ground mechanical room is furnished across its floor rather than only along its
perimeter.
